# Results R7: Auxiliary-Metal Site Effects and Co–N₄ Coordination Responses

Last modified: 2026-09-25

Hydrogen adsorption at Co was accompanied by electronic rearrangement throughout the Co–N₄ coordination unit. Across 129 paired records before and after adsorption (clean/H), covering 107 compositions, the Co $d_{z^2}$ projected population decreased in 128 pairs. The total d population increased in 127 pairs, and the absolute local Co moment decreased in 127 pairs, indicating a shared orbital-redistribution response (Figure S13a; SI Section S1.12). The coordinated N ligands also exhibited distinctly nonuniform charge changes. In the common Γ-point static comparison, the Co–N₄ unit of Co–Cu–Fe–Zn had an H-minus-clean Bader net charge change of −0.0178 e. The sum of absolute atomic changes nevertheless reached 0.2192 e, approximately twice the Ni value. Co and three N atoms lost electrons, while the fourth N gained electrons (SI Section S1.12). Compensation among atoms thus allowed substantial local electronic rearrangement to coexist with a small net charge change of the unit.

The coordination response also varied with the combination of auxiliary metals. Four compositions with one remote Zn fixed, Cu or Mn at the neighboring site, and Cr or Fe at the other remote site exhibited different Co–N bonding changes. In the standard basis, all 16 Co–N bonds had negative integrated crystal orbital Hamilton populations (ICOHPs) that shifted toward zero upon adsorption, indicating reduced bonding contributions. Summing the changes over the four Co–N bonds in each composition defines $B=\sum_{\mathrm{Co-N}}(\mathrm{ICOHP}_{\mathrm{H}}-\mathrm{ICOHP}_{\mathrm{clean}})$. Cr→Fe substitution decreased $B$ from 0.4418 to 0.3527 eV with Cu but increased $B$ from 0.5077 to 0.5355 eV with Mn (SI Section S1.12). The same remote substitution therefore reduced adsorption-induced Co–N bond weakening in the Cu environment but increased it in the Mn environment. Mean adsorption-induced Co–N elongation decreased with substitution in both environments (Figure S13b), so this common geometric trend accompanied opposite bonding responses.

The distinction between the two neighboring environments lay in the combination of s/p and d bonding contributions. Cr→Fe substitution changed the Co 3d contribution to $B$ by −0.04301 and −0.04475 eV with Cu and Mn, respectively, showing similar d responses. By contrast, the Mn-side 4s and 4p contributions changed by +0.04441 and +0.02794 eV. Together, they outweighed the negative 3d change and made the total change in $B$ positive. The Cu-side 4p change of −0.05402 eV instead reinforced the 3d change. The Mn-side directions were retained in a second basis containing 4p functions (Figure S13c). Neighbor-dependent s/p contributions reinforced or compensated the 3d change, accounting for the opposite directions of the total Co–N bonding response.

Differences in remote composition were also reflected in adsorption electronic energies at fixed geometries. The clean/H structures of Co–Cu–Cr–Zn and Co–Cu–Fe–Zn served as two reference geometries with fixed nuclear coordinates. For the respective converged electronic states, total energies excluding the entropy term gave $J=(E_{\mathrm{H}}-E_{\mathrm{clean}})_{\mathrm{Fe}}-(E_{\mathrm{H}}-E_{\mathrm{clean}})_{\mathrm{Cr}}$. The two reference geometries yielded $J$ values of +78.71 and +54.55 meV, both above the prespecified 10 meV threshold. Fe substitution for Cr thus increased the adsorption electronic energy at both geometries (SI Section S1.12). Meanwhile, the clean-state Co $d_{z^2}$ population was higher with Fe by 0.09469 and 0.08443 e, respectively. The summed population of the other four d orbitals decreased. The orbital-population differences and adsorption-energy changes at fixed nuclear coordinates show that remote composition can modify the electronic response of the Co center. The adsorption-energy response also depends on the converged electronic state (Figure S14; SI Section S1.12).

This distinction between site roles also informs an empirical expression based on elemental attributes. SISSO selected atomic radii and d-electron counts from the original 87-dimensional input while retaining the adsorption center $M_1$, neighboring metal $M_2$, and remote pair $\{M_3,M_4\}$. The empirical prediction for the signed hydrogen adsorption free energy is

$$
\widehat{\Delta G_{\mathrm{H}}}=b_{M_1}+a_{M_1}D_r+c_{M_1}D_e.
$$

(Figure 8a)

Here, $D_r$ and $D_e$ are the size and electronic components, respectively, defined as

$$
D_r=\frac{r_2+(r_3+r_4)/2+\lvert r_3-r_4\rvert-2r_1}{100\,\mathrm{pm}},
\qquad
D_e=(n_{d,2}-n_{d,1})\left[\frac{n_{d,3}+n_{d,4}}{2}-n_{d,2}\right],
$$

where $r_i$ and $n_{d,i}$ are tabulated atomic radii and d-electron counts, respectively. $D_r$ describes size differences between the center and its auxiliary environment, whereas $D_e$ combines center and remote d-electron-count differences relative to the neighboring site. Both components satisfy $M_3\leftrightarrow M_4$ exchange invariance, distinguishing neighboring and remote roles while preserving a symmetric representation of the remote sites (SI Section S1.12).

Across 995 compositions, the expression achieved macro-averaged MAEs of 0.1181 and 0.1184 eV in two composition-grouped cross-validation schemes, with each adsorption-center element weighted equally. Both errors were below those of the model with the same number of parameters but no neighboring/remote distinction, at 0.1328 and 0.1310 eV, respectively (Figure 8b; Figure S12a). Retaining site information thus improved the empirical description of overall free-energy trends. Among 110 historical supplementary records, 10 of the 22 compositions ranked closest to zero satisfied $\lvert\Delta G_{\mathrm{H}}\rvert\leq0.1$ eV. Using each adsorption-center element's median free energy as the reference gave an expected 10.2 qualifying compositions after accounting for ties (Figure S12b). In the Cr/Fe/Ni comparison with Co, Cu, and one remote Zn fixed, DFT and the empirical expression selected Fe and Ni, respectively (SI Section S1.12).

Auxiliary-metal effects in the Co–N₄/G systems examined thus depend on site assignment. The neighboring metal alters the relative s/p and d bonding responses, causing the same remote substitution to produce different Co–N responses. The site-resolved expression retains these positional distinctions and improves the description of overall free-energy trends. Compositional optimization of multimetal catalysts should therefore combine adsorption-center selection with joint consideration of neighboring elements and remote-metal combinations.
